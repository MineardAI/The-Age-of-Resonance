# Foundations

**Framing question:** What is the architecture?

This collection introduces the core conceptual and formal basis of the Resonance program. It is the best entry point for readers who want the program-level framing before moving into components or historical drafts.

## Recommended Read Order

1. [The Age of Resonance](../The%20Age%20of%20Resonance.pdf)
2. [The Architecture of a Noticing Machine](../The%20Architecture%20of%20a%20Noticing%20Machine.pdf)
3. [Mathematical Definition of Resonance](../Mathematical%20Definition%20of%20Resonance.pdf)
4. [From World Models to Noticing Machines](../From_World_Models_to_Noticing_Machines.pdf)

## Papers

- [The Age of Resonance](../The%20Age%20of%20Resonance.pdf) defines the broader research program and presents resonance as a way to organize coherent interaction across time, context, and persona. It sets the conceptual boundaries for the rest of the repository and is the clearest single starting point for the architecture as a whole.

- [The Architecture of a Noticing Machine](../The%20Architecture%20of%20a%20Noticing%20Machine.pdf) introduces noticing as an architectural primitive rather than a passive observation step. It explores how an AI system might maintain stateful awareness of interaction patterns while remaining grounded in responsive behavior.

- [Mathematical Definition of Resonance](../Mathematical%20Definition%20of%20Resonance.pdf) develops a formal vocabulary for resonance-related quantities and relationships. It is best read as the repository's mathematical backbone, especially for readers trying to separate metaphor from definitional structure.

- [From World Models to Noticing Machines](../From_World_Models_to_Noticing_Machines.pdf) traces a conceptual transition from world modeling toward interaction-centered systems. It helps explain why the program emphasizes noticing, state continuity, and response coherence instead of treating prediction as the only organizing principle.

## Relationship to the Other Collections

The Foundations collection gives the architectural frame. The Components collection elaborates mechanisms inside that frame, and the History collection preserves earlier terminology and developmental drafts so the program's evolution remains visible.
